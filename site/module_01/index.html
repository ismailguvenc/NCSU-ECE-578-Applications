The slide illustrates approximately 1,000× network area capacity through separate improvements in bandwidth, per-link spectral efficiency, and network density. The three multipliers are illustrative design factors, not universal forecasts or independent single-user channel gains.

Capacity improvement on a logarithmic scale

Aggregate area capacity ∝ available spectrum × spectral efficiency × spatial reuse density; single-link capacity ∝ available spectrum × spectral efficiency (subject to SINR and overhead).
Densification creates smaller cells and more spatial reuse, but also adds deployment cost, interference and handover/paging load. A 56× factor for density is not a promise of 56× throughput for one user.

Free-space path-loss calculator

Move the carrier frequency or separation to see why higher frequencies produce larger isotropic free-space path loss (FSPL). Ideal line of sight only: no buildings, foliage, rain, or reflections.

Isotropic FSPL—
Received power, 0 dBi antennas—
FSPL (dB) = 20 log₁₀(4πdf / c) = 32.44 + 20 log₁₀(f_MHz) + 20 log₁₀(d_km)
At a fixed distance and equal isotropic antenna gains, doubling frequency increases FSPL by 6.02 dB. Doubling distance also adds 6.02 dB. In a real link budget, antenna gains and other propagation losses matter.

Who shares the band with whom?

CBRS · 3550–3700 MHz

Three coordinated access tiers:

1. Incumbents: federal radar and other protected incumbent operations (including certain satellite earth-station uses)

2. PAL: priority licensed access, generally 3550–3650 MHz

3. GAA: lower-priority, nonexclusive access across 3550–3700 MHz

SAS coordinates frequencies; ESC sensing helps protect specified federal incumbents.

TV White Spaces · TV bands

Protected users: licensed TV broadcast, certain licensed wireless microphones and other protected services

Secondary opportunistic use: authorized white-space devices operate on location-dependent available channels

Mechanism: geolocation plus an FCC-approved database that determines channel availability

Availability changes by location and time. It is not equivalent to measuring instantaneous RF activity.
